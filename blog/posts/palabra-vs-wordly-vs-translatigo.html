Palabra vs. Wordly vs. Translatigo: How They Actually Differ

These three get compared because they're all "AI does the translation now," but they're not really solving the same problem. Palabra is for meetings and webinars, billed in hour bundles. Wordly is for events and conferences, and most people use it for live captions — text, not audio. Translatigo is for live calls, translating speech into speech at a flat per-minute rate. Picking between them starts with what you're actually trying to do, not which one is "best."

What each one is actually built for

Palabra sits in the meetings-and-webinars category: it prices in monthly hour bundles — $60 for 3 hours, $150 for 10 hours, $500 for 50 hours — with a custom quote above that for enterprise volume. Wordly is built for larger events and conferences, and its most-used feature turns a speaker's audio into live captions an audience reads on their own device, priced by hours and attendee count on a subscription. Translatigo is scoped to live calls — support, sales, one-on-one or small-group conversations — translating speech into spoken audio in real time, with no bundle or subscription to manage, just $0.07 per minute.

Pricing shape, side by side

Palabra's cost drops per hour as you move up its tiers, but only if your usage matches the bundle you bought — unused hours don't roll over, and going over means an upgrade or overage. Wordly's subscription model works similarly: you're paying for access sized to an event or a season of events, not per minute of actual talk time. Translatigo has no tiers to manage: the rate is the same whether it's one call or a hundred, so cost tracks usage exactly rather than needing to be estimated ahead of time.

Text vs. audio, one-to-one vs. one-to-many

The clearest technical difference between Wordly and the other two is output format: Wordly's core use case is text captions for an audience following along, while Palabra and Translatigo both output spoken audio for people actively talking. The clearest difference between Palabra and Translatigo is shape of use — Palabra is built around scheduled meetings and webinars with a defined attendee list, while Translatigo is built for the individual, often unplanned call that needs to happen right now, without setup.

Current limitations

None of these three tools substitutes for a certified human interpreter where one is legally required, such as court proceedings or medical consent conversations. Pricing details for Palabra and Wordly reflect what's publicly posted as of this writing — enterprise and custom tiers for both are quote-based, so exact costs at scale should be confirmed directly with each vendor.
